# TEC Register change note: 8–11 October 2026

**Source:** Archived daily NESO Transmission Entry Capacity (TEC) register CSV snapshots dated 8, 9, 10 and 11 October 2026. **Comparison:** 8 October versus 11 October. The 8/9 and 10/11 files are byte-identical respectively, placing the observed update in the 9–10 October interval.

## Headline findings

| Measure | 8 October | 11 October | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Raw register rows | 2,196 | 2,225 | +29 |
| Distinct projects (stage-normalised project number, fallback project ID) | 2,054 | 2,082 | +28 |
| Projects with at least one Gate 2 stage | 107 | 109 | +2 |
| Projects with highest gate Gate 1 | 749 | 796 | +47 |
| Projects without a gate | 1,198 | 1,177 | -21 |
| Stage-level capacity tagged Gate 2 | 15,789 MW | 15,987 MW | +198 MW |

**Gate progression among continuing projects:** 22 projects (7,519 MW project-total capacity) changed from unassigned to Gate 1; two storage projects (198 MW combined) changed from unassigned to Gate 2: **Uskmouth – New Connection (120 MW)** and **Stella Battery Energy Storage (78 MW)**. These are changes in recorded register classifications, not evidence that construction or energisation has occurred.

**Register entries:** 29 distinct projects appeared, of which 25 were classified Gate 1 and four unassigned; one zero-capacity Hinkley Point B entry disappeared. A new appearance in the register must not be confused with a newly conceived or newly approved project.

**Developer concentrations among newly appearing entries:** nine project names containing *Elmya* total approximately **5,400 MW of project capacity**, while 11 entries under **548 UK Investment Holdings Limited** total approximately **3,650 MW**. These are register observations, not a claim of ownership structure, secured connection capacity, construction progress, or net new capacity to the system. Project names and capacities require careful treatment where stages or multiple technologies are involved.

## Interpretation

The 10 October register revision combines **newly visible entries** with **reclassification of existing projects**. This matters because counting all Gate 1 increases as new projects, or treating every Gate 2-labelled project's full multi-stage capacity as gated, would misrepresent progression.

The movement of 22 existing projects from unassigned to Gate 1 is analytically distinct from the 25 newly appearing Gate 1 projects. The two unassigned-to-Gate-2 movements add 198 MW of stage-level Gate 2-labelled capacity in the compared snapshots.

A larger register is not equivalent to a larger *deliverable* pipeline. Dates, gates, status and project identity must be evaluated longitudinally, and the dataset is an administrative snapshot rather than a construction or investment tracker.

## Reproducibility and limitations

- Compared original user-archived NESO CSVs dated **2026-10-08** and **2026-10-11**, with **2026-10-09** and **2026-10-10** used to narrow the change window.
- Project key follows the repository's existing `tec_diff.py` convention: normalise `PRO-...` project numbers by stripping stage suffixes; fall back to Project ID when necessary.
- Highest recorded gate classifies a project for **project counts**; **Gate 2 MW** sums only row/stage capacity explicitly tagged Gate 2.
- Project totals use the highest cumulative total capacity per normalised project; **do not sum cumulative totals across stages**.
- Some entries have multiple stages or mixed technologies. Project MW must not be presented as technology-specific MW without further disaggregation.
- Snapshot comparisons do not establish when a project first applied, whether a project is commercially viable, or whether gate assignment guarantees delivery.
- No independently verified attribution of the register revision's cause is made.
